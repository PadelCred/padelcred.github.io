Privacy

PadelCred has no ads or tracking, and accounts are optional. Here's exactly what it stores and where.

What stays on your phone

Your scores, results, players, league, achievements, profile and friends list are saved inside the app on your phone. Nothing is uploaded to us or anyone else. Deleting the app or clearing your browser's data removes it.

Videos and photos

The camera and microphone are only used while you're filming. Videos and clips stay in the app until you save or delete them. When you save, a copy goes to your phone's own storage. Your profile photo is saved on your phone only.

What you choose to share

If you don't sign in, nothing leaves your phone unless you share it yourself, for example a share card or a video clip.

If you sign in

Signing in is optional. If you do, your results, profile and friends list are saved to your PadelCred account on Google Firebase, stored in London, so they sync between your devices.

Your profile is public to other signed-in PadelCred users. That means your name, username, photo, bio, padel details, socials, playing style, stats, achievements, Overall, ranking and your last 10 results (score, date, venue, and who you played with and against). Matches you play with connected friends are also sent to them, and matches tagged with a shared group are shared with its members, along with live scores while you play.

Your email address is never shown to anyone. It's only used to sign you in.

You can hide yourself from the rankings in Your data, and delete your account and everything in it at any time from Account and sync.

Other services the app uses

The app is hosted on Cloudflare Pages and loads its fonts from Google Fonts. Like any website, those services can see your device's IP address when the app loads. They don't receive any of your scores, videos or profile.

How ratings work

Your rating starts from your Playtomic level, or from 0.5 without one. Your rating goes up when you win and down when you lose. How far it moves depends on who you played.

Who you play matters

Your pair's strength is the average of your two ratings. Before each match the app works out how likely each pair was to win. Beat a stronger pair and you gain a lot. Beat a weaker pair and you gain a little. Losing works the same way in reverse.

Some examples

Two pairs both on 1,000: the winners gain 16 and the losers drop 16.

You're on 1,000 and beat a pair on 1,200: you gain about 24. Lose to them and you only drop about 8, because you weren't expected to win.

The most anyone can gain or lose from one match is 32.

Fair for everyone

Both players in a pair move by the same amount, and the other pair moves the opposite way, so points are swapped, never created. A single rotation set counts for half a match. Changing partners doesn't matter, because everyone has their own rating.

Padel rules, the short version

Scoring

Points go 15, 30, 40, game. At 40–40 your group decides: advantage (win two points in a row), golden point (next point wins) or star point (two advantages, then a deciding point). Six games wins a set, but you need to be two clear. At 6–6 there's a tiebreak to 7, again two clear.

Serving

Bounce the ball behind the service line, then hit it at or below waist height with at least one foot on the ground. Serve diagonally into the opposite service box. It must bounce in the box before it touches any wall. You get two attempts.

If the serve clips the net and lands in the box, replay it. If it then hits the metal fence, it's a fault.

Who serves

Teams swap serve every game, and partners take turns, so all four players serve in rotation. At the start of each set, a pair can change which of them serves first.

The walls

The ball must bounce on the ground on the other side before touching any wall or fence. After that bounce, it can hit the walls and you can still play it before it bounces a second time.

You can hit the ball against your own back or side glass to get it over the net. If your shot hits their wall or fence before bouncing, it's your point lost.

You lose the point if

The ball bounces twice on your side, you hit it into your own fence so it can't come back, you touch the net, or the ball hits you or your partner.

Changing ends

Swap sides after the first game and then every two games (after games 1, 3, 5 and so on). In a tiebreak, swap every six points. PadelCred tells you when.

These are the basics most clubs play by. Local rules can vary, so agree them before you start.

How ratings work

Your Overall

Your Overall is the big number on your profile card, from 40 to 99. It's 70% your results and 30% how the friends you play rate you after matches.

40–59 Rookie60–74 Club75–84 Pro85–99 Legend

Where you start

Your Playtomic level sets your starting Overall. No level? You start at 40, the same as Playtomic's 0.5.

Playtomic 0.5 or no level40
Playtomic 2.054
Playtomic 3.567
Playtomic 5.081

Your starting level locks when you play your first match, so it can't be changed to jump the rankings.

How results move you

After every match, points move from the losing pair to the winning pair. Each pair's strength is the average of its two players. The more surprising the result, the more points move:

Beat an evenly matched pairup a fair bit
Beat a much stronger pairup a lot
Beat a much weaker pairup a little
Lose to a much weaker pairdown a lot

So the higher you climb, the more you're expected to win. A round in a swapped-partners session moves half as much as a full match.

Ratings from friends

After a match, you can rate the other players from half a star to 5 stars. Tap a star once for half, twice for a full star. Ratings of 1 or below, or 5, ask you to double check they're fair. Ratings only count once at least 3 different people have rated you. The highest and lowest are dropped, and nobody ever sees who gave what, only an average.

Overall and rating

Your rating is the number underneath, shown in the League (it starts between 700 and 1,340 depending on your level). Your Overall is the same thing on a simple 40 to 99 scale, plus your friends' ratings. It moves by no more than 3 points a day, so one bad night won't wreck it.

Form

Form shows how you're playing right now, from 0 to 100. Each match gets a score: 50 means you played exactly as expected. Beating a stronger pair, or winning lots of games even in a loss, pushes it up. Your Form is a weighted average of your last 10 matches, with the most recent counting most.

Your Overall is your long-term level. Form is your current run. A Rookie can be on fire, and a Legend can be struggling.
